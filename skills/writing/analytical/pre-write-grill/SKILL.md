# Pre-Write Grill

Last updated: 2026-10-02

Make the difficult writing decisions while changing them is cheap. Grill against a concrete
framework the author can inspect, not an abstract questionnaire or a first draft. Use the
[shared brief](../../foundations/frame/references/brief-format.md) as the working record.

## Workflow

1. **Reconnoiter before asking.** Read the supplied inventory or existing material map and enough
   source content to identify competing explanations, tensions and possible contributions. Mark
   unread sources honestly. Material reading and `frame` are allowed during this conversation;
   final selection comes after framework agreement.
2. **Establish the question.** Reuse the author's reader, thesis, constraints and stated judgments.
   If the angle is genuinely undecided, offer a few concrete alternatives with their reader gain,
   reasoning burden and exclusions. Ask only about decisions the materials cannot settle.
3. **Build something to challenge.** Work with `frame` and `develop-argument` to present a provisional
   thesis, section claims, justified connections, boundaries and original contribution in Markdown.
   Use a diagram when it reveals dependencies. Keep this at statement/outline level: no opening,
   sample paragraphs or article prose. There is no separate mandatory specification document.
4. **Probe the weakest important decision.** Ask a focused question, explain what it changes, then
   revise the framework using the answer. Useful probes include:
   - What does this conclusion explain that a summary of the sources would not?
   - Which premise carries the conclusion, and what would falsify or narrow it?
   - What is the strongest alternative explanation? Where do the two make different predictions?
   - Which section changes the reader's understanding, and why must it precede the next one?
   - Does the proposed synthesis reflect the author's judgment, or is it still an AI proposal?
   Select relevant probes; do not mechanically ask every question or force a disagreement.
5. **Close the actual uncertainty.** Continue until the framework has a defensible contribution,
   enough reasoning to earn it, and no unresolved author-intent conflict. `develop-argument`
   presents the completed framework and records checkpoint 1. Accept confirmation already given
   or explicit delegation covering the decisions. A vague response is not silent approval.

## Boundaries and handoff

This skill owns the questioning, not the logic or evidence tables. `frame` records framing;
`develop-argument` records the reasoning and framework checkpoint. Use the checkpoint rules in the
shared contract so the author is not asked to approve the same decision by every skill.

A rough length constraint can guide feasible scope here. Detailed source priorities, excerpt
choices, example treatment and section word counts belong to `develop-examples` after checkpoint 1.
Research at that stage answers named evidence gaps. During framework work, state provisional
premises and available support without pretending they have been verified.

An analytical task needs this reasoning process even if all its material is in one file. A faithful
summary, standalone edit, or already settled framework does not require a fresh grilling session.
On a standalone grilling request, return the tested framework and unresolved decisions without
starting material selection or drafting.

## Contract test

Given scattered notes, produce a provisional reasoning structure before asking for agreement.
Given a shallow list of headings, expose the missing inference or contribution. Given a confirmed
framework or explicit editorial delegation, reuse it. No article prose or duplicate specification
is produced, and no source selection is prematurely treated as final.
